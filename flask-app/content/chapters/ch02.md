## BAsh (or *The Bourne Again Shell)

The Bourne Again Shell (hereafter, *bash*). Why is it born again? Why is it spelled like that?

Ten layers of irony.

Bash was created in 1989 by Brian Fox. It built on the work of a Bell Labratories researcher Stephen Bourne (hence, Bourne *again*). The original shell (or *sh*) was Portable Operating System Interface (POSIX)conforming, meaning that it conforms to a set of standards across all UNIX devices. Though this is beyond the scope of this class, suffice to say that when you see POSIX, think "scripting and basic utilities. Bash continues to be the default shell installed in every distro as it is part of the GNU kernel files. However, like all 40 year old technology there have been innovations due to how users have decided they want their shells to behave; the most popular competitior being <a href="https://ohmyz.sh/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" aria-label="navigate to the z.sh homepage">z.sh</a>. Shell come in different shapes and sizes and colors and functionality. When deciding on a shell to use, factors one should use are considerations like purpose. Bash is as popular as it is due to its incredible performance, requiring the use of very little memory and CPU. Because of this it can work much faster than a heavier shell with more user comfort features. We can discern the difference between bash and something like z.sh like the difference between a military High Mobility Multi-purpose Wheeled Vehicle (HMMWV) and a commercial-grade Hummer H3. That HMMWV is meant to perform under war-time conditions and to always plug along on the battlefield, but it isn't considered fun to drive, or comfortable by any means. While the H3 is a comfortable ride meant for creature comforts. They may look the same, but they are built with different purposes in mind.
